Chapter five · The day we install

What happens the day they put it on?

Most NC roofs go on in a single day. Some take two. This chapter is the hour-by-hour walkthrough we wish we could hand every customer the week before — what to do, what to expect, what's normal, and what isn't. The job is on us. The peace of mind is what we owe you.

01

The week before.

Install day is our responsibility, not yours. But there's a short list of things that make it go smoother — and a longer list of things we'll handle so you don't have to.

What to do

Six small things, the week before

  • Move cars off the driveway and out from under the eaves the night before
  • Cover landscaping you're protective of with a tarp — or tell us, we'll tarp it
  • Take down loose pictures and shelves on top floor walls (vibration)
  • If you store anything in the attic, cover it — some dust will sift through
  • Give a heads-up to immediate neighbors. Tear-off is loud for ~2 hours.
  • Plan for pets indoors — the noise and the dumpster bother them
What you don't need to do

Things we handle — not you

  • Be home. We don't need you there. We'll send photos.
  • Provide a bathroom. We bring our own portable.
  • Provide power, water, or food. We bring it all.
  • Clean up after us. We sweep the yard twice with magnets.
  • Move outdoor furniture, grills, or pots — we'll move and replace them.
  • Worry. Your foreman calls or texts you with updates twice during the day.
02

The day, hour by hour.

Most NC residential roofs are tear-off-to-cleanup in a single working day — about 9 to 10 hours from arrival to handoff. Here's exactly what happens, in order.

6:30
AM

Foreman call

Your foreman texts or calls to confirm arrival time. If weather pushed us to a different day, you'd have heard from us by 5:00 PM the night before.

7:30
AM

Crew arrives, sets up

Trucks stage along the curb. Dumpster placed on driveway or street with plywood under the wheels. Tarps spread to protect plants, AC units, and the walkways.

What you'll hear: truck doors, ladders going up, crew talking. Loud but ordinary.
8:00
AM

Tear-off begins

The old roof comes off. Shingles, underlayment, old flashing — everything down to the wood decking. This is the loudest hour or two of the day. Pets should be inside.

What you'll hear: sustained pounding from above, shingles hitting the tarps and the dumpster. Vibration in the house.
11:00
AM

Decking inspection & underlayment

With the old roof off, we inspect every square foot of the decking. Soft, rotted, or damaged sheets get replaced now — this is what the optional "decking replacement" line item in your estimate is for. Then synthetic underlayment goes on, and ice & water shield in valleys and at penetrations.

If decking needs to be replaced: the foreman texts you a photo and the cost before we cut it. No surprises on the invoice.
12:00
PM

Crew lunch

Short break. Crew eats at the trucks — not in your house. We don't ask for access to your kitchen, bathroom, or property other than what's needed to work.

1:00
PM

Starter strip & shingles

Starter strip goes on the eaves and rakes. Then shingles, working from bottom to top in full courses across the roof. This is the longest phase of the day — quieter than tear-off but still constant nail-gun work.

What you'll hear: rhythmic nail-gun snaps. Less vibration than tear-off.
4:00
PM

Flashing, ridge cap, ventilation

Custom-bent metal goes around the chimney, valleys, and any skylights. Ridge cap (specialized shingles) caps the peak. Ridge vent and soffit balance get final adjustment.

5:00
PM

Cleanup & magnet sweep

Tarps come down, debris goes in the dumpster. Two crew members sweep the entire yard with heavy magnets to pick up every stray nail. Outdoor furniture, planters, AC units back in place.

The magnet sweep: we do the yard twice. If you find a nail in the driveway a week later, call us — we come back and re-sweep, no charge.
5:30
PM

Walkthrough & handoff

Your foreman walks the perimeter of the house with you. Drone photos of the finished roof on a tablet for the parts you can't see from the ground. Punch list signed. Warranty packet handed over. Done.

03

What's normal — and what isn't.

Some things look or sound alarming on install day but are completely routine. A few things would actually warrant interrupting the foreman. Here's the difference.

Normal · don't worry

Things that happen on a good install day

  • The house shakes during tear-off — vibration is normal
  • Drywall cracks or picture frames shift on upstairs walls
  • Dust sifts down from the attic into closets and upstairs rooms
  • The dumpster sits in your driveway overnight if the job ends after pickup hours
  • You see a torn shingle or two in the dumpster — we cull defects on the ground
  • The yard looks worse before it looks better — cleanup happens at the end
Concerning · tell the foreman

Things that warrant a conversation

  • Anything broken — window, light fixture, AC condenser fin
  • A specific worker behaving inappropriately or unsafely
  • The crew leaves for more than 90 minutes without explanation
  • You see materials being installed that aren't what you bought
  • The foreman won't show you photos of the decking before re-covering
  • You feel like something is wrong — trust that feeling, ask
04

End-of-day handoff.

Before the crew leaves, your foreman walks the property with you and hands you a packet. Here's exactly what should be in it.

What we hand you before we leave.

If your foreman tries to leave without going through this list with you, stop them. This is the closing step, and it's where most "after the install" problems get prevented.

  • Drone photos of the finished roof from every angle, on a tablet you can review
  • Warranty packet — manufacturer warranty registration, our workmanship warranty, all in writing
  • Final invoice with any decking-replacement or unforeseen-work line items itemized and explained
  • Punch list — anything we still owe you (touch-up paint, a replacement gutter clip, etc.) with a date we'll be back
  • Direct phone number for your foreman and the office, in case something comes up in the next two weeks
  • Magnet sweep receipt — we sign off that we swept the yard twice; if you find a nail, we re-sweep free
05

If it goes more than one day.

Most NC roofs finish in a single day. Some don't — usually because of weather, an unexpected decking issue, or a complex shape. Here's how that goes.

Whenever we can't finish in a day, the roof is left dried in — meaning the underlayment is fully installed and weather-tight even if no shingles are on yet. Your house is sealed from rain before the crew leaves. We don't go home with an open roof.

Day two usually starts later than day one (around 8:30 AM) because we don't have to tear off or stage materials. Shingles go on, flashing finishes, cleanup happens. Same end-of-day walkthrough as a single-day job.

If weather pushes day two further out, the dried-in roof is fine in NC weather for up to two weeks — the underlayment is rated for it. We've watched plenty of dried-in roofs sit through hurricanes without an interior leak. Call us if it's been more than a week and you haven't heard from your foreman.
