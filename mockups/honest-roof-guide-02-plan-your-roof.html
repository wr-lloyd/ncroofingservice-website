01

What a roof actually does.

Two jobs. Everything else is a variation on these two.

Job one is to keep water out of your house. Not just rain coming straight down — rain blowing sideways at 50 miles an hour, snow that melts and refreezes at the eaves, water that runs down the brick and finds the smallest gap in the flashing.

Job two is to keep your attic the right temperature. A roof that doesn't breathe properly cooks your insulation in summer, traps moisture in winter, and shortens its own life by ten years. Most of what makes a roof last is invisible — ventilation, underlayment, and what's around the chimneys, valleys, and vents. Shingles are just the lid.

Keep those two jobs in mind as you read the rest of this chapter. A "fancier" roof that doesn't do them better than a basic one isn't worth the upgrade. Most NC homes are fine with the boring answer.

02

The three materials you'll actually choose between.

NC residential roofs are 95% one of these three. The other 5% — tile, slate, cedar shake — we're happy to talk through if you're curious, but they don't come up most of the time.

The default · 80% of NC roofs
Architectural shingle
also called "dimensional" or "laminated"
$9k – $16k typical 2,000 sq ft NC home, installed
Lifespan 25 – 30 years
The honest workhorse. Looks like real shingle, not the flat 1990s kind. Comes in a dozen colors. Properly installed and ventilated, this is the right answer for most NC houses.
Best for
Most houses. Most budgets. Most people.
Trade-off
Less dramatic curb appeal than premium options.
The upgrade · stay-forever pick
Premium / designer shingle
heavier, looks like wood shake or slate
$14k – $24k typical 2,000 sq ft NC home, installed
Lifespan 30 – 50 years
Thicker, heavier, with more visual depth. Real upgrade in curb appeal and storm resistance. Costs more not just to buy but to install — they take longer to lay and nail.
Best for
Older homes, larger homes, owners staying 15+ years.
Trade-off
You may not recover the premium at resale.
Once-in-a-generation choice
Standing seam metal
aluminum, steel, or copper panels
$24k – $45k typical 2,000 sq ft NC home, installed
Lifespan 40 – 70 years
Long, vertical metal panels with hidden fasteners. Sheds snow and water beautifully, handles wind, and is genuinely a once-and-done roof for a healthy house. Looks distinctive — which some HOAs love and some don't.
Best for
Forever homes, rural / mountain properties, modern designs.
Trade-off
Upfront cost. Noisier in heavy rain. Not always HOA-approved.

03

The nine components of a real roof.

A roof is a system, not a stack of shingles. Here's every component we install on every replacement — what each one does, and why skipping any of them is how leaks happen later.

1

Roof Deck

The structural base (plywood or OSB) that everything attaches to. Must be solid, dry, and properly fastened.

2

Drip Edge

Metal flashing along eaves and rakes to direct water away from the deck and fascia, preventing rot.

3

Ice & Water Shield

Self-adhering waterproof membrane in valleys, eaves, and penetrations to prevent leaks from ice dams and wind-driven rain.

4

Underlayment

A protective layer over the entire deck (synthetic or felt) providing secondary water resistance beneath the shingles.

5

Flashing

Custom-installed metal at penetrations and transitions (chimneys, walls, skylights, valleys) to keep water out.

6

Starter Strip

The first row at eaves and rakes that seals the roof edge and helps shingles resist wind uplift.

7

Shingles

The primary weather-shedding layer and most visible component — what most people think of as "the roof."

8

Ventilation

Balanced airflow (soffit + ridge vents) to regulate attic temperature, prevent moisture, and extend roof life.

9

Ridge Cap

Specialized shingles at the roof peak to protect the ridge and allow proper exhaust ventilation.

!
Why this matters.

Most low-bid replacement quotes skip 3 or 4 of these components — usually the underlayment grade, the ice & water shield, the starter strip, or the ventilation balance. The roof still looks finished from the ground. The leak shows up in year three.

Worth knowing about: the Fortified roof.

It's a real certification (from the Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety) that means your roof was installed to higher wind and water standards. In coastal and high-wind NC counties it often reduces your homeowner's insurance premium — sometimes enough to pay for the upgrade itself.

04

What it really costs in North Carolina.

Real numbers from real 2025 jobs in our service area. Range-bound, not single numbers — because every roof is different and any roofer who quotes you a single price without looking is making it up.

Simple roof, good shape

Single-slope or simple gable, no decking issues
$9,000 – $13,000 Architectural shingle, ~2,000 sq ft, easy access, no decking replacement. Usually a single-day job.

Complex roof, normal shape

Multiple slopes, valleys, dormers
$13,000 – $20,000 More flashing, more cuts, more time. Same shingle, but the labor and materials around chimneys, valleys, and skylights drive the price.

Steep pitch or rough decking

Adds safety equipment or new wood
+ $2,000 – $6,000 Steeper than 8:12 needs harness rigging and more crew time. Decking replacement is per sheet — usually 2 – 12 sheets on a 2,000 sq ft house.

Upgrade to premium or metal

Stepping up from architectural shingle
+ $5,000 – $25,000 Premium designer shingle adds ~$5k – $8k. Standing seam metal adds $15k – $25k. Both significantly extend roof life.

These numbers come from real 2025 jobs in Charlotte, the Triangle, and the Blue Ridge. The only way to know your number is for one of our roofers to come look. The visit is free — we'll tell you the range before you decide anything.

05

Five questions that get you to a real answer.

Read these. Answer them honestly to yourself. By the end you'll know which conversation to have — not just which roof to buy.

1

How long do you plan to live in this house?

Not "forever" or "I don't know." Pick a number you'd bet a hundred dollars on.

Less than 5 years. Don't over-invest. Architectural shingle is almost certainly your answer. — 5 to 15 years. Architectural is still usually right. — 15+ years. Premium or metal starts to pay off.
2

Is your current roof under 12 years old?

Look at your settlement papers from when you bought the house, or ask the previous owner. Most NC homes have records.

Yes. You may not need a replacement at all — see Chapter 01's repair-or-replace table. — No. Replacement is on the table for real.
3

Are you in a high-wind or coastal NC area?

Outer Banks, anywhere east of I-95, parts of the Blue Ridge ridgeline, or an area with active hail history.

Yes. Fortified upgrade is worth a real conversation. Premium shingle or metal also makes more sense here. — No. Standard install is fine — make sure your installer uses the storm-resistant nailing pattern by default.
4

Does your HOA or neighborhood restrict materials or colors?

This shows up in older planned communities, gated neighborhoods, and historic districts. Check your covenants — not the rumor mill.

Yes. Get the approved list before you decide. Metal is the most commonly restricted. — No. Choose on the merits.
5

What matters most: lowest cost now, lowest cost over 30 years, or insurance / resale?

Be honest with yourself. None of these is wrong — but they lead to different roofs.

Lowest now. Architectural shingle, plain installation, defer the upgrades. — Lowest over 30 years. Metal or premium shingle starts to win. — Insurance / resale. Fortified certification and the documented 9-component install matter more than the material itself.
