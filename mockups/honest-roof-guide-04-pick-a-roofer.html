01

The five non-negotiables.

Before you ever talk price, verify these five things. Any roofer (including us) who can't cleanly show all five should not be in your driveway.

NC general contractor license

Look it up on the NC Licensing Board for General Contractors site. Real license, current status, no complaints. Takes 30 seconds.

General liability + workers' comp

Both insurances. Ask for a Certificate of Insurance with your name on it. Without workers' comp, you're liable if someone gets hurt on your roof.

Manufacturer certification

Certified by the shingle brand they're installing (GAF, CertainTeed, Owens Corning). Required for the long warranties to apply. Verifiable on the manufacturer's site.

Physical NC address

An office you could drive to. Not a PO box. Not a virtual address. Storm chasers operate out of trucks. Honest roofers operate out of buildings.

Written estimate, itemized

Before any work starts. Materials, labor, the nine components we install, total. Not a one-line "ROOF REPLACEMENT - $X" scribble on a business card.

References they didn't pick

See the last section in this chapter. Asking for references is fine. Drive by three of their recent jobs at random is better.

03

Red flags & storm chasers.

Storm chasers follow weather events across state lines. They knock on doors, sign quick contracts, do quick work, and leave. By the time you notice the problems, they're three states away. Here's how to spot one before they pick up the pen.

!
Ten red flags. Any one is reason to slow down. Two or more is reason to walk.

If you see any of these from a roofer in your driveway or on the phone, treat it as a signal. Honest roofers don't do these things — not because we're virtuous, but because the math doesn't work for a business that intends to be here next year.

  • Knocked on your door uninvited
  • "Just happened to be in the area"
  • Promises a "free roof" or to "cover your deductible"
  • Wants the contract signed today
  • Asks for full payment up front
  • Cash-only or no written estimate
  • Out-of-state phone number or license plate
  • Truck has only magnetic logos (not painted)
  • Can't show you a certificate of insurance
  • Asks you to sign an Assignment of Benefits

Storm chasers prey on urgency. The cure is time. There is almost no roof problem that can't wait 48 hours while you check credentials, get a second opinion, and call the people who answer their phones in an office — not from a truck on the highway.

05

The reference check that catches everything.

Every roofer hands you the same three happy customers. The references they pick aren't going to tell you the truth. Here's the one that works.

Drive by three of their finished jobs at random.

Ask any roofer for the addresses of their last 20 NC jobs. Pick three at random. Drive past them at a random time. You're looking for:

  • Yard signs or branded vehicles in the driveway (they're proud of the work)
  • Clean-looking installation from the street — ridge cap straight, flashing flush, no obvious sag
  • No nails in the driveway, no shingle debris in the bushes
  • If a homeowner is outside, say hi. Ask: "How was the install? Would you hire them again?" Real answer beats curated testimonial every time.

If the roofer hesitates to give you that list — or only gives you handpicked names — that's its own answer. Our list is posted on our work page. Every job. Address, date, materials, before and after.
