02

Insurance, honestly.

Insurance is the most powerful and the most misunderstood payment path. Three things insurance companies don't go out of their way to tell you — and two phrases from a contractor that mean walk away.

1
The truth

One weather claim won't raise your rates in NC.

North Carolina law restricts insurers from raising premiums or dropping policies based on a single weather-related claim. Many homeowners avoid filing because they're afraid — and pay out of pocket for damage that was covered. Read your policy. Or call us — we'll read it with you.

2
The truth

The first adjuster usually misses things.

Adjusters cover dozens of houses a week. They miss damage. They underestimate the code upgrades NC building code now requires. A real roofer can file a supplement — an additional claim for things found during tear-off or required by code. Most homeowners have never heard the word.

3
The truth

ACV vs. RCV is the line that decides what you actually get.

Actual Cash Value pays you the depreciated value of your old roof. Replacement Cost Value pays you to actually replace it. On a 15-year-old roof, ACV might pay $4,000 for a $15,000 replacement. RCV pays $15,000 minus your deductible. Two letters in your policy decide a $10,000 difference. Check.

!
Two phrases from a contractor that mean walk away.

"Your insurance will cover everything — you pay $0."   In NC, a contractor offering to waive your deductible is insurance fraud. The deductible is your share of the claim. A "free roof" promise is either a misunderstanding of the law or someone who doesn't care about it. Either way, walk.

"Just sign this Assignment of Benefits."   An AOB hands your insurance check — and your authority to negotiate with the insurer — to the contractor. They can settle without you, even sue your insurer in your name. Reputable NC roofers don't ask for this. We don't.

03

Cash, financing, or both.

The non-insurance paths are simpler, but the financing one has the most predatory marketing of anything in our industry. Here's what's real.

Path A · Cash

Paying out of pocket.

If you have the reserves, this is almost always the cheapest way to get a roof. No financing fees folded into the price. No interest. No paperwork after we leave.

Most roofers (us included) will take $500–$1,000 off the cash price — we save the financing-partner fee and pass it back.

  • Keep at least 3 months of emergency reserves intact
  • Pay in two installments — deposit and completion — not all up front
  • Never pay a roofer the full amount before work starts
Path B · Financing

Spreading the cost.

Three real flavors: HELOC (lowest rates, requires equity), roofer-arranged financing through a partner like GreenSky or Service Finance (faster, higher rates), and personal loans / credit cards (slowest payoff, highest rates — usually the wrong tool).

Watch the "0% APR for 18 months" trap. If you don't pay it off by month 18, deferred interest accrues from day 1. A $15,000 roof can balloon by $3,000+. Always read the deferred-interest clause.

  • Get the all-in cost over the full term in writing
  • Confirm there's no pre-payment penalty
  • HELOC almost always beats roofer financing if you qualify

04

Which path is right for you?

Read these. Answer them honestly. By the end you'll know which conversation to have first — even if it's with your insurance agent, not us.

1

Was there a storm or sudden event in the last 12 months?

Hail, high wind, a tree branch, anything that could have caused damage.

Yes. Insurance is in play. Start there — even if you think it won't pay out. The Storm Check tool shows what actually hit your address. — No. Skip to cash or financing.
2

Pull up your homeowner's policy. Does it say ACV or RCV?

It's in the declarations page, usually under "Coverage A" or "Dwelling." If you can't find it, your agent will tell you.

RCV (Replacement Cost Value). Insurance pays the full replacement cost minus your deductible. Strong case to file. — ACV (Actual Cash Value). Older roofs may get depreciated heavily. Run the math before deciding.
3

How much of the cost can you put down comfortably?

"Comfortably" means without touching your emergency fund.

All of it. Cash path. Ask for the cash discount. — Half. Cash + short-term financing for the rest. — Just the deductible. Insurance + financing for the gap.
4

Do you have a HELOC, or could you qualify for one?

Home equity line of credit. Usually 7–9% interest, much lower than roofer financing.

Yes / could qualify. This is almost always the lowest-cost financing option. Worth the extra paperwork. — No. Roofer-arranged financing is next best. Avoid personal credit cards.
5

When does it need to happen?

Be honest with yourself. An active leak is now. A 20-year-old roof on a dry day isn't.

Active leak right now. Move fast. Finance if cash isn't liquid. Call us. — This year. Take time. Get quotes. Plan. — Eventually. Save up. Do it on your terms.
